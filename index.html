Study 01 · on main Credit

Statlog German Credit · scikit-learn

Credit risk decisions, not just a leaderboard

0.772
Holdout ROC-AUC for the shipped gradient boosting model. Selection used training-fold ROC-AUC, 0.793 ± 0.054, not this number.
8.9%
Holdout default rate inside the booked loans, 5 of 56, against a 30% base rate. The cutoff books 56 of 200 applicants.
$22,800
Planning cost of that book. It is the Statlog matrix at $200 a unit, not expected loss and not a P&L.
Problem
Who to approve when funding a defaulter is treated as five times as costly as turning away a borrower who would have paid.
Data
Hofmann, Statlog German Credit (1994), read as OpenML credit-g. 1,000 installment applicants, 20 attributes, no missing values, 300 defaults. personal_status mixes sex and marital status. foreign_worker encodes nationality. Both stay in the benchmark. A second cross-validation drops them.
Evaluation
Stratified 80/20 split, seed 42: 800 train, 200 holdout. Model class and cutoff are fit on the training rows only. Five-fold stratified CV picks the highest mean ROC-AUC. Each model’s cutoff minimizes Statlog cost on out-of-fold training scores. The winner is refit on the 800 and scored once on the 200.
Architecture
One scikit-learn pipeline each for logistic regression, a random forest, and histogram gradient boosting: impute, scale numerics, one-hot categories, balanced class weights. Gradient boosting is selected. The desk cutoff is 0.15. Because the weights are balanced, 0.15 is not a 15% probability.
Tradeoff
The boosting margin over the forest on training-fold AUC is about 0.0005, inside a fold standard deviation near 0.05. Holdout cost prefers the forest ($20,400) and the untuned logistic ($22,000). A tuned logistic baseline, C = 0.1, has holdout ROC-AUC 0.809 and illustrative cost $21,000. The README reports those gaps and does not use them to replace the shipped booster. Against approving everyone, the 0.15 rule’s illustrative gap is $37,200. A 0.50 cutoff costs $27,200 on the same 200 people.
Limits
Dropping the two demographic columns moves boosting’s mean CV ROC-AUC from 0.793 to 0.786. That is not a fair-lending validation. On training out-of-fold scores at cutoff 0.15, female-labeled applicants are approved 9.4 percentage points less often than male-labeled applicants, almost entirely extra false denials, and applicants under 25 are approved 20.6 percentage points less often. The README says those gaps are not a fair-lending finding. Holdout Brier is 0.187, against 0.210 for predicting the 30% base rate for everyone. This is a 1994 German installment file, not a 2026 U.S. application population.
Tests
On main, GitHub Actions runs Ruff and pytest. The README requires at least 95% line coverage of src/credit_risk. The suite includes the cost arithmetic and the saved-model scorer and does not need a network call.

App URL, not verified as public. The README links credit-risk-prediction.streamlit.app. On 6 Nov 2026 an unauthenticated request was redirected to a Streamlit sign-in page. The still and GIF on this page are the files committed on main.

Baseline, bootstrap, and the desk, on main

Merged in pull request 3, commit e4a0933. A logistic baseline is allowed a penalty search the race models do not get. C is chosen on the training folds from 0.1, 1, and 10. The winner is C = 0.1, training-fold ROC-AUC 0.781 ± 0.057. The README says that CV number can look a little better than a nested search. The baseline is not allowed to replace boosting after the holdout is opened.

A stratified percentile bootstrap of the out-of-fold scores, 1,000 replicates, seed 42, does not refit the model. Boosting minus that baseline is +0.010 ROC-AUC, 95% interval −0.010 to 0.031, which includes zero. The out-of-fold Brier difference is −0.011, interval −0.020 to −0.002, which excludes zero. Out-of-fold cost per applicant does not separate them. The holdout price gap of $1,800 is reported and is not a reason to change the shipped model.

The README’s desk, on this same commit, scores one file, scores a CSV of up to 5,000 rows, draws bootstrap intervals, and moves a cost slider. Profit on that slider means illustrative dollars saved versus approving everyone, at $200 a cost unit. It is not revenue. The sign-in redirect above means this page does not claim a public browser can open that desk.

What the write-up can defend

Why 0.15 is a desk cutoff and not a probability. Why boosting shipped when the holdout cost preferred the forest. What $22,800 is, and why it is not recovered cash. Why the demographic columns can come out, and why that sentence is not a fair-lending sign-off.

Study 02 · on main Lending

Lending Club sample · OpenML 43729

Loan approval prediction

0.703
Calibrated holdout ROC-AUC, 95% interval 0.674 to 0.734. Selection used cross-validated ROC-AUC, 0.670 ± 0.015, not this split. Uncalibrated ranking AUC is 0.704.
94.9%
Holdout precision at the calibrated cutoff 0.89: 415 of 437 approvals were fully paid. The rule approves 22.8% of 1,916 loans.
$139,000
Planning-cost gap versus declining everyone. The scored book costs $826,400, decline-everyone $965,400, approve-everyone $1,535,000. Not a lender’s P&L.
Problem
Fund a loan or turn it away before repayment is known. Fully paid is the positive class. The planning weights are $5,000 for a false approval and $600 for a false decline.
Data
Public Lending Club sample, OpenML data id 43729, CC0. 9,578 loans, 13 predictors in the cached file, 8,045 fully paid (84.0%). The deployed score does not see credit_policy or int_rate, because both already contain a lender’s judgment. The extract has no sex, age, or race fields.
Evaluation
Stratified 20% holdout, seed 42 (1,916 loans). Model choice and the cutoff use the other 7,662 only. Logistic regression and histogram gradient boosting are compared on the same 5 stratified folds. A paired bootstrap, 1,000 resamples, seed 42, must exclude zero before a more complex model replaces the simpler one. The holdout bootstrap is reported and is not used to choose. After the choice, isotonic regression is fit on out-of-fold scores only, and the cutoff minimizes planning cost on those cross-fit probabilities.
Architecture
Preprocessing sits inside the pipeline, so each fold refits it: median impute and scale for numerics, most-frequent impute and one-hot for purpose. Logistic regression is deployed (C=10, balanced class weight). Its cross-validated ROC-AUC is 0.670 ± 0.015 against 0.658 ± 0.012 for histogram gradient boosting. The out-of-fold gap, logistic minus boosting, has a 95% interval of 0.003 to 0.021, which excludes zero. The same gap on the holdout is 0.006, interval −0.010 to 0.022, which includes zero and was not allowed to pick the model.
Tradeoff
F1 of 0.406 is what a strict cutoff looks like when most fully paid loans are declined on purpose. The deployed book funds 22 loans that are not fully paid and books 415 that are. The planning save is $72.55 a loan versus declining everyone. Shuffling FICO drops holdout ROC-AUC by about 0.078. Putting credit_policy and int_rate back changes cross-validated ROC-AUC by about +0.001.
Limits
Before calibration the holdout Brier is 0.223, worse than guessing the 84% fully-paid rate (0.135). After the isotonic map it is 0.124, so the calibrated score beats that constant. The cutoff 0.89 sits next to the 0.893 break-even of the two weights. It is still a planning cutoff, not a charge-off probability from this file. The file has no term and no loss amount. Segment rates by FICO and purpose are screening arithmetic, not a protected-class result. The extract has no sex, age, or race fields.
Tests
On main, pytest fails if coverage of loan_approval drops under 95%. GitHub Actions runs Ruff and pytest on Python 3.12 from the cached CSV. It does not retrain and it does not call OpenML.

App URL, not verified as public. The README links loan-approval-prediction.streamlit.app. On 6 Nov 2026 an unauthenticated request was redirected to a Streamlit sign-in page.

Merged in pull request 3, commit 25bd00c. The deployed probability is the isotonic map, not the balanced class-weight score. What the write-up can defend

Study 03 · on main Fraud

OpenML credit card · data id 1597

Credit-card fraud review lab

0.679
Test PR-AUC for the selected random forest, 95% interval 0.579 to 0.788. The fraud rate is 0.172%. Accuracy is not the objective.
76 of 95
Test frauds sent to review, with 25 legitimate tickets pulled. Precision 0.752 and recall 0.800 at the cutoff locked on validation.
$10,903
Planning save versus approving every test transaction, rounded to the nearest dollar. In reports/metrics.json on main the forest test cost is $6,411.164 and approve-everything is $17,314.518. That difference is $10,903.354, which rounds to $10,903. Rounding each cost first ($6,411 and $17,315) and then subtracting gives $10,904. The 95% interval on the forest’s test cost is $2,102–$12,180. The README’s $4,828 is the missed-fraud principal on 19 test frauds ($4,828.164), not the principal of approving every fraud ($14,939.518).
Problem
Which tickets to pull into a manual review queue. After exact duplicates are removed, approving every transaction is already 99.83% accurate, and it still pays every fraud.
Data
OpenML creditcard, data id 1597. Fingerprint 284,807 rows and 492 frauds. 9,144 exact duplicate rows removed, then 275,663 modeling rows and 473 frauds. Columns used are V1–V28 and amount. Time is not in this file. Amounts are converted at 1.10 dollars per euro before the fit. That rate is a planning input, not a 2013 spot rate.
Evaluation
Seed 42: train 165,397, validation 55,133, test 55,133, with 95 frauds in each of validation and test. Duplicates are dropped only after the fingerprint matches, so a copied ticket cannot sit in both train and test. The selection rule is locked before the test set: lowest validation dollar cost, ties broken by validation PR-AUC. Cutoffs minimize validation cost. The test set is scored once.
Architecture
Class-weighted logistic regression, an undersampled logistic regression, a random forest, histogram gradient boosting, an isolation forest fit only on legitimate training rows, and an amount-only rule. Amount is log1p-scaled with a scaler fit on the training fold. The forest wins validation cost. Its cutoff is 0.413574. The queue is about 18.3 reviews per 10,000 transactions.
Tradeoff
On the test set an undersampled logistic regression costs $5,728 and has PR-AUC 0.713, against the forest’s 0.679 and $6,411. It was not selected. The README records selection_matches_test_leader: false. A class-weighted logistic regression left at 0.50 costs $26,933, worse than approving everything. The same scores at the validation cutoff cost $6,105. Median fraud is $10.80 and median legitimate spend is $26.13, so “review the large tickets” is aimed at the wrong place. The amount rule’s best validation policy is to approve everything.
Limits
Ninety-five test frauds make a $1,000 gap look sharper than it is. Review is assumed to save the full principal. Costs are planning assumptions: $8 review, $12 false decline, $25 chargeback, on top of principal. V1–V28 are principal components. The lab does not name the original fields, and it does not brief them as merchant categories. There is no protected-class audit in this file.
Tests
On main, GitHub Actions runs Ruff and pytest on a synthetic generator, including a forced OpenML failure, so CI does not download the public file. Coverage of fraudlab fails under 95%. The published scorecard itself is from the verified public extract, not from that synthetic run.

App URL, not verified as public. The README links fraud-detection-ml.streamlit.app. On 6 Nov 2026 an unauthenticated request was redirected to a Streamlit sign-in page.

Alert budget and monitoring, on main

Merged in pull request 3, commit 5ec1eec. The scorecard was not refit. The random forest is still the validation-cost choice. The README adds a planning volume of 137,831.5 transactions a day, from two days in the extract and no timestamps to split them. At 237 review slots a day the cached validation grid expects 182.5 frauds caught and $19,684 of loss. That is the validation rate times a planning volume. It is not a calendar day, and it is not the test result of 76 frauds out of 95.

Random-half PSI on the validation scores is 0.0002. The README calls that two halves of the same book, not a drift event. A PSI above 0.25 is described as a common review trigger, not a number fit on this file. Batch CSV scoring, up to 2,000 rows, is in this commit. Revenue reconciliation

Before the daily forecast, the same public extract is classified in SQLite: raw text, one disposition per row, then a daily mart. Merged in pull request 4, commit 9668ce57. £9,762,760.55
Net merchandise in the committed snapshot, 1 Dec 2010 through 8 Dec 2011, integer pence. Gross sales £10,072,920.35 plus cancellations −£310,159.80. The quality check requires that identity.
£11,629.44
Separate forecast. Holdout MAE for the random forest on that daily series, 1 Oct 2011 through 8 Dec 2011. About £654 under the same-weekday-last-week forecast (£12,283.78). Not a change in revenue.
Pipeline
Full refresh into raw, staging, and mart. Disposition priority drops an incomplete final day, unparseable values, non-product codes, and non-positive prices before an exact-duplicate extra can be flagged. The file has no FX rates and no payment date, so there is no currency conversion and no receivables aging. Published files are aggregates. Customer ids stay out of this copy.

02What the repos show

Practices that are visible in the public studies. No proficiency bars, and no claim of a production seat.

Decision models

scikit-learn pipelines with the preprocessor inside the fold. Logistic regression, random forests, and histogram gradient boosting, plus an isolation forest kept as a late-label baseline on the fraud study. Cutoffs are chosen on out-of-fold or validation cost and then frozen. The write-ups report ROC-AUC or PR-AUC, a proper scoring rule where they computed one, and the cases where the holdout ranking disagrees with the precommitted choice.

Evaluation hygiene

Stratified or chronological splits, seed recorded, selection rules written before the holdout. Public files named with citations: Statlog German Credit, Lending Club OpenML 43729, OpenML credit-card 1597, UCI Online Retail. pandas for the tables. pytest, Ruff, and GitHub Actions on the three flagship repositories. Main now includes the bootstrap intervals, the calibrated loan cutoff, the fraud planning volume, and the generated model cards from the merged pull requests.

Finance context

The studies are written as desk decisions: a 5:1 credit matrix, a $5,000 / $600 loan scenario, and a review-queue cost account. Separately, the public profile lists tax preparation, QuickBooks Online as a QuickBooks ProAdvisor, and Double Eagle Financial LLC for small-business bookkeeping. HTML, CSS, and JavaScript for the marketing samples. Go High Level is what the profile names for the talking-website samples.
